Stem Cells, Exosomes, and NK Cells: Three Different Fields

A practical introduction to three biological categories that are often grouped together but should never be treated as interchangeable.

7 min read · Educational information only

Why the distinction matters

Stem cells, exosomes, and natural killer cells describe different biological entities. Stem cells are living cells with self-renewal and differentiation properties. Exosomes are extracellular vesicles released by cells. Natural killer cells are immune cells. A claim, specification, or body of evidence about one category cannot be transferred to another.

Stem cells are not one uniform product

Stem cell products can differ by cell source, processing, identity, viability, purity, dose, storage, intended use, and regulatory pathway. Those differences affect how a specific product should be evaluated, transported, administered, and discussed.

Exosome research is not product approval

Exosomes are being studied in many research contexts, but scientific interest does not establish that every commercial exosome product is safe, effective, authorised, or suitable for human use. Product source, isolation, characterisation, composition, quality controls, and intended use require careful review.

NK cells belong to immune-cell science

Natural killer cells are part of the innate immune system. NK-cell therapies and products sit within a specialised professional context. They should not be marketed as simple immunity boosters, and a visitor should never use general information to select a cell product or dose.

Questions for a product-specific conversation

Qualified partners should establish the exact product identity, manufacturer, intended use, audience, regulatory status, manufacturing controls, handling requirements, supporting evidence, and current approved documentation before drawing conclusions.
